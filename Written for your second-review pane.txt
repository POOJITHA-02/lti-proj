### Slide 1 — Introduction
**Signature-Driven Inventory: stocking for the shopper who is actually in the aisle**
- Stores usually order stock from the average of recent weeks' sales.
- Averages hide **who** is buying and react **late** to sudden changes in demand, so shelves run empty or excess stock piles up.
- This project learns shopper behaviour from real baskets, spots demand spikes early, and forecasts how much of each product to stock.
- Dataset: Instacart Market Basket (real, anonymised grocery orders):
  - **3.3 million orders, 33.8 million order lines**
  - **206,213 shoppers**
  - **49,688 products, 21 departments, 134 aisles**

### Slide 2 — Objectives
- Profile every shopper and basket on five behaviours: single or family, top-up or stock-up, ready-to-eat or cooking ingredients, routine or exploring, small or large basket.
- Discover shopper groups automatically with deep clustering, and compare the models.
- Detect demand peaks from each product's own sales history, without a festival calendar.
- Prove the value: compare against traditional stocking on real orders.
- Deliver it as a live, deployable web app with plain-language, AI-written summaries checked against the numbers.

### Slide 3 — Modules
1. **Data engineering:** loading, cleaning, live order stream
2. **Feature engineering:** product tagging and basket behaviour rules
3. **Pattern discovery:** deep clustering into shopper groups
4. **Demand peaks and product relationships:** peak detection and a co-purchase graph
5. **Value proof:** inventory backtest against traditional stocking
6. **Web app and deployment:** dashboard, timeline, AI insights, Docker

### Slide 4 — Methodology / model planning
- **Pipeline:** raw orders → weekly timelines → behaviour labels → shopper groups → demand peaks → forecast → stock decision
- **Behaviour labels:**
  - Transparent rules, with each indicator casting a weighted vote.
  - Every label carries a confidence and the evidence behind it.
- **Shopper groups:**
  - Seven models trained on the same inputs.
  - The best is chosen by its average rank across quality metrics.
- **Peak detection:**
  - Each product's share of weekly sales is compared with the median of its previous 8 weeks.
  - A week counts as unusual when z ≥ 3 and the rise is at least 25%.
  - Peaks are typed as one-off, lasting or repeating, and as affecting one product, a category or the whole store.
- **Value test (walk-forward backtest):**
  - Each week, each method sees only the past.
  - Both methods use the same ordering rule and the same weekly self-tuning.
  - They are compared at the same 97% of orders filled.

### Slide 5 — Data preprocessing & EDA
**Preprocessing**
- Loaded the Kaggle CSVs into SQLite and indexed them for fast queries.
- The data has no calendar dates, so dates were reconstructed from each shopper's gaps between orders.
- Aggregated to weekly totals for every product, aisle and department, excluding partial and low-volume weeks.
- Kept 5,237 products with enough sales to track weekly.
- Tagged all 49,688 products by rules plus an AI model.
- Fields the data doesn't have (quantity per item, prices, pack size) are marked "not available" and never estimated.

**EDA findings**
- Fresh produce is about 30% of all items; dairy and eggs about 17%.
- An average basket holds about 10 items.
- Total orders swing by more than 25% within weeks.
- 489 demand peaks in one year.
- 6 shopper groups. The largest (81% of shoppers) is everyday families. The niche groups buy far more of one department than the average shopper: pet supplies 31×, alcohol 23×, baby products 16×.

### Slide 6 — Approach / algorithms / tools
**Algorithms**
- Rule-based weighted voting (basket behaviour)
- Deep clustering: GCN (selected), DGCformer, DEC, IDEC, contrastive clustering, transformer, with k-means as the baseline
- Clustering metrics: silhouette, Davies–Bouldin, Calinski–Harabasz, stability across runs (ARI)
- Live group assignment: k-nearest neighbours, agreeing with the batch model for 98% of held-out shoppers
- Robust anomaly detection: median baseline with z-scores
- Forecasting and stocking: moving average and exponential smoothing as the traditional baselines, against the shopper-signature forecast; order-up-to policy with a safety margin

**Tools**
- Python: pandas, NumPy, SciPy, scikit-learn, PyTorch, UMAP
- Storage and app: SQLite, FastAPI, Chart.js
- AI summaries: LLM via Groq / Gemini, with automatic fact-checking
- Deployment: Docker, GitHub Actions, Caddy (HTTPS)
- Reporting: Power BI
